Energy Storage Optimization

A home battery is most valuable when it stores cheap or solar energy and discharges during expensive peak hours.

Charge and discharge windows:
- Charge from rooftop solar first. If solar is not enough, charge from the grid only in off-peak hours, usually late night to early morning.
- Discharge during on-peak windows, often 4 PM to 9 PM on weekdays, to cover HVAC, cooking, and lighting.
- Keep a reserve, commonly 20-30%, for outages unless a storm is not expected.

Pairing storage with solar and EVs:
- Do not charge the EV and the house battery from the grid at the same time during mid-peak or on-peak rates.
- If the EV can wait until overnight, let the house battery serve the evening peak and charge the car later.
- On a sunny weekend, fill the battery by mid-afternoon, then run the pool pump and laundry from solar instead of draining the battery too early.

Sizing the opportunity:
- Shifting 10 kWh from on-peak ($0.33/kWh) to off-peak ($0.13/kWh) saves about $2.00 that day, or roughly $50-70 per month if repeated on weekdays.
- A battery used only to back up rare outages has a weaker bill ROI than a battery that cycles on time-of-use rates every weekday.
- Round-trip efficiency is often 85-90%. Include that loss when comparing solar self-consumption vs export.

Operating rules:
- Avoid frequent tiny cycles that add wear without reducing peak import.
- Update the schedule when the utility changes peak hours.
- During multi-day cloudy weather, prioritize off-peak grid charging over waiting for weak solar.
- If a heat wave is forecast, pre-charge the battery the night before so evening air conditioning does not buy high-price grid power.
