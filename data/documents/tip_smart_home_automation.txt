Smart Home Automation Tips

Home automation is most useful when it follows electricity prices, occupancy, and solar output instead of a fixed clock.

Thermostats and occupancy:
- Geofencing can set an away mode when nobody is home and recover before arrival.
- Occupancy sensors should not fight the HVAC schedule. Use a 15-30 minute delay before changing modes.
- Sleep schedules should pre-condition the house before peak rates, then relax setpoints overnight.

Appliance and EV automations:
- Put dishwashers, washers, dryers, and EV chargers on delayed start. Trigger them at off-peak rates or when solar export is high.
- A typical dishwasher cycle uses about 1.2-1.8 kWh. Moving it from 6-8 PM peak to overnight off-peak can cut the cycle cost by 40-60% on time-of-use rates.
- EV charging should finish just before departure. Overnight off-peak plus midday solar is usually cheaper than 4-9 PM charging.
- Smart plugs can cut phantom load from TVs, game consoles, and chargers when the home is asleep.

Lighting and water:
- Occupancy lighting and dimming reduce wasted lighting energy.
- Hot water recirculation pumps should run in short occupied windows, not 24 hours.
- Leak alerts on water heaters and washing machines prevent hidden energy and water waste.

Control rules that work well:
- If solar irradiance is high and prices are mid-peak, run flexible loads now.
- If prices are on-peak and solar is low, delay flexible loads.
- If outdoor temperature is extreme, pre-heat or pre-cool before the price spike.
- Keep a manual override. Automation should never strand an EV below the needed charge.
