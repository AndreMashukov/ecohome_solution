Renewable Energy Integration

Homes with rooftop solar save the most when flexible loads follow the sun and remaining grid use stays in off-peak windows.

Self-consumption first:
- Midday solar is usually strongest from about 10 AM to 3 PM on clear days. Shift EV charging, pool pumps, dishwashers, and laundry into that window when people are home.
- Cloudy and rainy hours cut solar yield sharply. On those days, prefer overnight off-peak grid charging instead of waiting for weak midday sun.
- Compare forecast irradiance with recent generation history. If yesterday produced little energy under similar clouds, do not count on a large surplus today.

Inverters, export, and interconnection:
- Many utilities pay less for exported solar than they charge for imported energy. Using solar on-site is usually more valuable than exporting it.
- Avoid running large loads after sunset unless rates are off-peak. After 4 PM, solar is falling while residential prices often rise.
- Keep panels clean enough that soiling does not become obvious. Heavy dust or pollen can reduce output by 5-15%.
- Trim shade from trees on the south and west roof edges where allowed.

Coordinating solar with devices:
- EV: charge during high-irradiance hours if the car is parked at home, otherwise charge overnight off-peak.
- HVAC: pre-cool with solar-powered air conditioning before late-afternoon peak prices.
- Pool pump: run the main filtration cycle during the sunniest 6-8 hours, then idle overnight.
- Water heating: if a heat-pump water heater or timer is available, recover tanks during solar surplus.

Weather-aware planning:
- A sunny forecast with high irradiance is the best day to do laundry, charge the EV, and run the pool pump.
- A multi-day cloudy stretch is the time to conserve HVAC and delay optional loads until overnight off-peak.
